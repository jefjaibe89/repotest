Why this exists

vManage answers "what is configured", not "what is happening"

01

It is a configuration tool

Its job is pushing templates and policies. Monitoring arrived afterwards, and it shows: the state is there, but scattered and without hierarchy.

02

The answer lives on five screens

Reachability on one, BFD on another, OMP on the third, alarms on the fourth. Nobody cross-references them at three in the morning.

03

The history does not reach back

"Did this start today or has it been a week?" is the first question of any incident, and it is exactly the one that cannot be answered.

The product

History

We keep the score and the device-down count with your own retention. Windows of 1 h, 6 h, 24 h and 7 days, to tell a one-off spike from a degradation that has been running for days.

Alerting

It tells you once, not a hundred times

Each alert carries a stable key: a device down for an hour produces one message, not one per poll. With a recovery notice when it clears. Slack, Teams or a plain webhook.

Detail

One click on any device opens its interfaces, IPsec tunnels, control connections and received OMP routes. The whole path without changing tool.

Architecture

Load does not grow with viewers

One collector queries the controller on a fixed cadence and every browser reads from there. Ten screens in the NOC weigh on vManage exactly what one does.

Deployment

What everyone asks

The reasonable doubts, answered

We already have vManage. Why another tool?

Because they solve different things. vManage is where you configure: templates, policies, device onboarding. This is where you look. It does not replace it or compete with it — it feeds off its API. If your team already cross-references five screens by hand to answer "is it fine?", that is the task that disappears.

Our management network has no internet access

It is built for that. It loads no CDN libraries and depends on no external services at runtime: everything is served from the deployment itself. That is why the dependencies ship bundled instead of being downloaded when the page opens.

Where does our data end up?

Self-hosted, in your infrastructure and nowhere else: the history is stored in a local database belonging to the deployment. A network inventory is sensitive information — device names, addresses, firmware versions, sites — and we treat it as such. On the managed option we state in the contract where it lives and how long it is kept.

What if we do not use the 20 hours in two months?

They are lost, and we would rather say so plainly than bury it in the fine print. The window exists so the work gets done while the context is fresh, not so we keep the money: if you renew the pack, unused hours roll into the next one. If you can see you will not get there, tell us and we will find something worth spending them on.

How long until it is running?

Against a reachable vManage, the first screen with real data lands the same day: it needs a read-only account and network reach to the controller. Calibrating thresholds and alert rules to how you operate takes longer, and that is precisely what the hour pack is for.

Does it change anything on our network?

No. It only reads from the vManage API. It pushes no templates, changes no policies and touches no devices. A read-only account is enough, and it is the one we recommend using.
